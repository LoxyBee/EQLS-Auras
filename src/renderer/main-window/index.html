What the app thinks you have memorized

Checking...

Built from "You forget X." / "You have finished memorizing X." lines as you swap gems, and remembered between sessions. It is not read from your spellbook file, and the app never replays log history - so if you change gems while it's closed, this will still be showing the old loadout. Click any gem to forget it when that happens; a wrong entry here makes the app ignore buffs it should be tracking. The log never says which slot a spell went into, so positions won't match your in-game bar - regular spells fill from the left, bard songs (underlined) from the right.

        Spellbook: Checking...

        Your character's own spellbook is used to resolve ambiguous buff messages (EQ reuses a lot of generic text across many different spells) down to whichever one you actually have. Read from your EQ install folder above.

        This is worth fixing. EverQuest reuses the same buff message across many different spells, and without your spellbook the app cannot tell which of them was yours - so it ignores them. On one recent session that was over 700 buff messages thrown away.

        Detection log

        Live feed of every buff/aura decision - self and ally buffs landing, expiring or being ignored, and every custom trigger firing, ending or being restored after a restart - and why. Useful for understanding a confusing moment without digging through the raw log yourself. The same lines are saved to a file, one per day, so you can go back to a moment that has already scrolled past.

        Memorized/forgotten spells

        Live feed of ONLY "You forget X." and "You have finished memorizing X." lines - the two lines that build the "currently memorized" picture detection relies on. Nothing else shows here, so it's a fast way to check whether something you expect to change gems (a loadout swap, in particular) actually printed anything at all - confirmed 25 Aug that a full loadout swap can print nothing, which is exactly why this exists: that gap would otherwise be invisible in the middle of everything else the regular log feed and detection log show.

        Evidence-based detection

        Changes one thing: when a buff's landing text is unique but it looks like it's not currently memorized by you (or an ally's instant-grant burst just fired), the app no longer silently drops it. Instead it asks you, the same way any other ambiguous cast does - because "not seen loaded this session" can go stale (a loadout swap, or the app restarting mid-session) and isn't real proof the buff wasn't yours. Off by default. If this misbehaves, turn it back off - nothing else changes when you do.

        Experimental

        Cast-time-aware confirmation

        Changes one thing: after a named cast begins, the app waits for its own scaled cast time (from the roster, adjusted for the spell's rank) instead of a flat 12 seconds for every spell regardless of how fast it actually casts - plus half a second of slack for log-timestamp rounding. Falls back to the flat 12 seconds for anything the roster has no cast time for, so nothing is ever worse off than today. The per-rank scaling rates come from the mote spreadsheet and are not independently measured against your own logs the way some duration rates are. Off by default. If this misbehaves, turn it back off - nothing else changes when you do.

        Experimental

        Self-buff overwrite detection

        Changes one thing: EQ prints an explicit line when a buff on someone ELSE gets overwritten, but nothing at all when it happens to one of your own buffs - so two spells that share the same fade text (e.g. Nimble and Agility both say "Your agility fades.") can leave a stale timer on screen for whichever one didn't actually end. When this is on, the moment a new buff lands that the game's own stacking rules say would replace one you already have active, the stale one is removed immediately instead of waiting on an ambiguous fade line. Computed from the same spell data the game itself uses - not a guess - but only for the core same-slot rule; some rarer interactions (bard songs, damage-over-time, group-vs-single-target versions) aren't covered yet and simply won't be caught either way. Off by default. If this misbehaves, turn it back off - nothing else changes when you do.

                  About

                  How auras work

                  An aura is an overlay window with its own position, size, and buffs. Manage them under "Overlay Auras" in the sidebar - click one to configure it, or "+ Add aura" to import a share code, add a premade, or build a custom one. "Self Buffs" is built-in and can't be deleted. EQ must run windowed or borderless-windowed (not exclusive fullscreen) for auras to show over it.

                  How loadout profiles work

                  If your server lets you swap multiclass loadouts without touching your spellbook, the app can't detect the swap - so a remembered "this text means buff X" answer can quietly become wrong. Profiles fix this by giving each loadout its own separate memory - switch profiles yourself (bar at the top) whenever you swap loadouts, since nothing does it automatically. Profiles also control which auras are visible: set this per aura under "Loadout profiles..." in its own settings; an aura with none ticked shows on every profile.

                  How a buff gets identified, most to least certain

                  EverQuest has no universal "buff landed" message, and a lot of landing text is shared across multiple spells - so the app narrows down which buff it actually was, in this order:

                  1. You just cast it by name. The next landing line confirms it.
                  2. The landing text is unique to one buff. Confirmed with no other context.
                  3. Ambiguous text, but only one candidate is memorized right now.
                  4. Ambiguous text, but only one candidate is anywhere in your spellbook.
                  5. Ambiguous text during a burst (e.g. Quick Buff) - presumed yours, since you triggered it.
                  6. Ambiguous text with no other signal. Presumed someone else's buff landing on you - hidden by default (opt-in on the Setup page).
                  7. Still ambiguous? You get asked - in the Ambiguous Casts panel or a popup near the overlay. Your answer is remembered until your group changes or you clear it under "View remembered choices."

                  Report a problem

                  Copies the app version and today's detection log (if Diagnostics has been turned on under the Log page) to your clipboard, ready to paste wherever you report bugs - the detection log is what actually makes a report diagnosable, since it records the exact decision the app made and why.
